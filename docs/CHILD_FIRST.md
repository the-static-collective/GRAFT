# Child-First Protection Layer

Black Star's child-first layer protects **memory, provenance, and the child's future relationship to their own history**.

It is not a parent-ranking system, custody predictor, diagnosis engine, or automated legal-strategy tool.

## Governing idea

A child should not become an evidence object merely because adults disagree.

The system therefore preserves:

- ordinary life before anyone knows what will later matter;
- plans separately from occurrences;
- observations separately from claims;
- direct statements separately from paraphrase;
- multiple adult perspectives without forcing premature reconciliation;
- carrier provenance and discovery chronology;
- memory-safe material for future family artifacts;
- adult-conflict material behind a separate privacy boundary.

## Core laws

**CHILD != EVIDENCE OBJECT.**

**PLANNED != OCCURRED.**

**PRESENCE != PARTICIPATION.**

**DIRECT STATEMENT != ADULT PARAPHRASE.**

**DIVERGENT ACCOUNTS MAY COEXIST; DIFFERENCE != VERDICT.**

**NO RECORD != NOTHING HAPPENED.**

**MEMORY RENDERING != SOURCE.**

**CHILD-FACING OUTPUT != ADULT-CONFLICT DOSSIER.**

**PARENTING-RELEVANT != LEGALLY SIGNIFICANT.**

**MEMORABLE != MATERIAL.**

## Why this improves evidence integrity

An archive assembled only after conflict begins has a selection problem: it naturally overrepresents disputed or adverse events.

Prospective ordinary-life capture changes the epistemic surface. The archive can preserve school days, meals, handoffs, jokes, appointments, missed plans, completed plans, boring afternoons, photographs, and conflicting recollections under one provenance grammar.

That still does not make any later claim true. It does make the archive better able to answer:

- What was being recorded before this became disputed?
- Why was this carrier preserved?
- Which parts were contemporaneous?
- Which accounts disagree?
- Which later relation was born only after new material arrived?
- Is a current narrative selecting only one class of events?

## Parallel realities

Child-first records may carry structured assertions from multiple observers. Black Star can show that two accounts differ on a field such as time, place, or sequence.

It does not choose a winner.

`blackstar child-perspectives record.json` returns:

- aligned assertions;
- divergent assertions;
- single-perspective assertions;
- the observer associated with each value.

That creates an early-warning surface for adults who are building incompatible narratives without turning the software into an adjudicator.

## Memory lane

`blackstar child-memory record.json` produces a **memory packet**, not finished prose.

The memory packet:

- includes only explicitly memory-eligible accounts;
- excludes adult-conflict material;
- excludes allegations and administrative records;
- keeps direct child statements marked as exact or paraphrased;
- carries a private provenance appendix.

A scrapbook renderer can later transform that packet. It should never need to read the adult-conflict layer.

## Prohibited machinery

Child-first records mechanically reject fields used for automated ranking such as:

- parent score;
- fitness score;
- custody score;
- truth score;
- credibility score;
- risk score;
- best-parent selection;
- legal-significance score.

Black Star may preserve source-specific safety concerns as attributable records. It does not turn them into an automatic parent verdict.

## Child future agency

The privacy block includes an explicit future-child-access state. This is intentionally not the same thing as current adult access.

The long-term product direction should support:

- material meant for the child's future self;
- material withheld pending later review;
- redacted descendant renderings;
- provenance-preserving corrections;
- explicit access-policy birthdays.

The child is the future owner of a life history, not merely the subject of somebody else's case file.
